# Regional MODFLOW 6 qualification

The three available Zell–Sanford workspaces ran unchanged in native MF6 6.6.2 and passed DarcyWarp's independent FP64 mass-balance checks on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. The imports used the original initial heads, with CHD values applied by each engine. Native heads were used only in the comparison stage.

## Native references and equation audit

The archived primary listing identifies MF6 6.0.4 and records 20 outer iterations, 98 inner iterations and 48.418 seconds on the original machine. A rebuild of the official 6.0.4 release with GNU Fortran reproduced the 20 and 98 iterations on this machine in 15.803 seconds. MF6 6.6.2 took 16.851 seconds and 19 outer / 103 inner iterations. The two reruns differ by 0.116 mm head RMS, with a 36.3 mm maximum. Both used the archived NEWTON UNDER_RELAXATION formulation and original IMS settings. The 6.6.2 run used the COMPLEX preset, BICGSTAB, ILUT with five levels and a drop tolerance of 0.0001, and 0.1 m inner and outer head-change tolerances.

A separate 6.6.2 copy enabled NPF `SAVE_SATURATION`. Its head file matched the ordinary run exactly. The tight reference used the same physical inputs with `OUTER_DVCLOSE 1e-8`, `INNER_DVCLOSE 1e-9` and `INNER_RCLOSE 1e-7 STRICT`. It converged in 61 outer and 1,305 inner iterations in 103.602 seconds. The native diagonal residual RMS decreased from 0.01197 to $1.44\times10^{-9}$ m³/day. The observed runtime difference includes the different IMS work and is specific to this model. [USGS model archive](https://www.usgs.gov/data/modflow-6-models-used-simulate-long-term-average-surficial-groundwater-system-contiguous) · [MF6 6.0.4 release](https://github.com/MODFLOW-ORG/modflow6/releases/tag/6.0.4)

The independent FloPy audit found zero maximum absolute differences in the active masks, geometry, initial heads, conductivity, ICELLTYPE, recharge, CHD, GHB and DRN inputs passed to the primary DarcyWarp operator. K ranges from 1.884 to 1,000 m/day, layer thickness from 5 to 51.413 m, GHB conductance from 15,625 to 146,875 m²/day and DRN conductance from 1,217 to 2,937,500 m²/day. The audit matched all 2,281,733 physical faces in both native CSR directions, including rows bordering IDOMAIN exclusions. List budgets use full-grid user cell IDs. Native zero exchange confirmed CHD suppression for all 1,944 GHB overlaps and 809 DRN overlaps. Native RCHA also has zero exchange at CHD. Each DRN record retained its own elevation and conductance.

MF6 uses harmonic full-thickness conductance times the quadratic saturation fraction of the upstream cell. The quadratic smoothing width is $10^{-6}$ of aquifer thickness. MF6 updates its saturation array when assembling coefficients and can write that earlier saturation alongside the final heads. Reconstructing native flows with saved `DATA-SAT` reduced the maximum face error from 3.50 to $2.33\times10^{-10}$ m³/day on the ordinary run. The CPU and CUDA recomputations from the final heads agreed to $4.55\times10^{-13}$ m³/day maximum face error. On the tight native heads, recomputed and native face flows differed by $1.20\times10^{-10}$ m³/day RMS. These realizations have a single layer, so their audit exercises horizontal faces. ## Numerical cause and changes

The original regional solve reproduced a scaled initial residual of 180.53266 and relative imbalance of 1.45016. With the original five-level current-coefficient preconditioner, the true residual was 180.51795 after 96 Krylov iterations. FP64 factor storage and uncaptured execution gave the same result. A longer restart, saturated coefficients and a deeper V-cycle with additional coarse sweeps left the residual above 178. Diagonal preconditioning brought it to 10.40 in 96 iterations, and current-coefficient Picard reached 1.54 in 20 iterations. In a 128 × 128 window spanning 7,157 irregular active cells and conductivities from 6.58 to 1,000 m/day, the production CUDA JVP agreed with the sparse CPU Jacobian to $1.41\times10^{-16}$ relative L2. A centred finite difference across the original active-set thresholds gave $1.93\times10^{-5}$ relative L2.

The current-coefficient preconditioner previously omitted the diagonal from the upstream saturation derivative. At the same initial state, adding that term reached the 1% linear target in 35 iterations. On the extracted window, a one-level FP32 smoothing cycle matched independently assembled weighted Jacobi to 6.0e-8 relative L2. The finest preconditioner diagonal matched the independently assembled Newton diagonal to $4.66\times10^{-10}$ m²/day maximum absolute error. The temporary continuation shift brought the initial linear solve to the same target in 21 iterations. The shifted linear system has the form $(J+\sigma I)\Delta h=R$. Residual and budget evaluations remain on the unmodified MF6 equations. The shift decays with physical residual RMS and is zero during final refinement and acceptance.

A second failure came from source-free cells approaching the exact dry endpoint. Several rows had sub-$10^{-16}$ m³/day imbalance but reported tens of metres of head-equivalent error when divided by a vanishing conductance. Even the tight native solution exceeded 0.23 m RMS on this metric. Candidate heads within the existing head tolerance can be evaluated at the exact cell bottom. Both evaluators still check the original balance. The independent topology check recognises a dry singleton only when its derivative, residual, source and package exchange are all exactly zero. Wet floating components and dry rows with nonzero forcing still fail.

At the recorded regional qualification revision, the importer enabled the qualified controls for CUDA workspaces with at least 100,000 active cells, all convertible, in one steady layer with GHB and DRN. This uses the existing five-level hierarchy, a restart of 16, and the current-coefficient Newton diagonal. The initial shift is 8% of median full-thickness $K_x b$, and the head-step cap is five median layer thicknesses. Mixed-fast still uses FP32 K-cycle state and FP64 heads, physical fluxes, Krylov reductions and acceptance. Explicit solver controls take precedence. FloPy also needs a compatibility step for the other two workspaces: a temporary copy moves the native-valid trailing MXITER option before IMS6 and then restores external-array resolution to the archived path.

The subsequent layered implementation generalises the same steady numerical
starting profile to convertible steady structured DIS models, without a
cell-count, device, package or layer-count restriction. The diagonal of the
current-coefficient Newton preconditioner is corrected by default, including
transient NPF. The continuation shift is scaled from median convertible-cell
horizontal conductance in both directions; it remains a steady-only correction,
not a physical storage term. Vertical connections are retained in drying
safeguards. See [MF6 import](MF6_IMPORT.md) for effective controls and audit
gates. The large-model timing results below describe the recorded one-layer
qualification revision; do not treat them as new multilayer benchmarks.

## Canterbury evidence

The project checkout is `Canterbury_transmissivty_and_stream_depletion`. Its archived `forward_final.json` and field bundle trace the run to `QUICK_FLOW/Canterbury_transmissivity/run_forward_model.py`. That script prepends the QUICK_FLOW repository and imports its vendored DarcyWarp `WarpDarcySolver`. At forward-driver commit `84175d5`, the vendored solver was last changed by `3867f33`. The solver blob `b9365a9ded2d7452cdcab0001b0edc3597a2a21e` is identical to the DarcyWarp blob at `aa32a5f`. The archive records settings and convergence but has no Git hash for the executing checkout, so the matching blobs establish the historical implementation available to the driver.

The recorded 250 m solve has a 1,424 × 1,458 grid and 723,317 active cells. It used a heterogeneous prescribed transmissivity field, a 1–300 m GHB thickness field, and GHB conductance formed from transmissivity and a calibrated conductance factor with spatial overrides. The initial head was the coast datum on active cells. QUICK_FLOW requested FP64 on CUDA and solved a linear system with CUDA graph reuse. The three levels used 2 × 2 coarsening and active-mask-aware block averaging and bilinear transfer, 13 weighted Jacobi sweeps before and after the coarse correction, and three PCG sweeps at the coarsest level. The first 10 cycles took 0.169 seconds. The warm refinement took 45 cycles and 0.695 seconds, reaching $2.86\times10^{-5}$ flow RMS at a relative tolerance of $2.5\times10^{-6}$. The recorded build plus solve took 1.59 seconds.

Canterbury's fixed transmissivity and linear GHB produce a symmetric, repeatedly reusable operator. Zell–Sanford multiplies face conductance by current upstream saturation and has a switching drain derivative. The existing CUDA hierarchy, graph reuse and coarse approximation transfer to the regional preconditioner. A separate fixed-transmissivity initialisation would approximate a different system and is unnecessary for the three tested runs, which use the archived heads directly.

## Reproducing and interpreting the benchmarks

Run [the regional benchmark](../working_tests/benchmark_regional_mf6.py) from the DarcyWarp root with `python -m working_tests.benchmark_regional_mf6`. Set the paths and stage switches in its main block. Native execution retains the listing and binary files in a fresh directory. The translation-only, native-head audit, importer solve, accepted-head comparison and results table can run independently. The audit writes numeric differences, mapped fields and per-layer PNGs, and rejects failed translation, physical equation and solution comparison gates. Retain native_heads.json and solution.json with the original native run receipt to support the benchmark table. Failed importer steps retain `darcywarp_rejected.npz` with heads and residuals, and the JSON report contains their solver history.

The tested inputs are all part of the one-layer Zell–Sanford model family. The initial linear sweep is a bounded comparison at the same original head state. It measures the preconditioner mismatch and the effect of the Newton diagonal at the starting state. The native production runs also reach different accuracy from DarcyWarp. Numerical solve time, cached end-to-end time and fresh kernel compilation are reported separately.
